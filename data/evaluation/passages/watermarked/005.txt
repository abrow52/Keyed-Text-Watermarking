I do suppose she is a Papist! The French generally are," said Aunt Priscilla, drawing her brows in a delicate sort of frown, and sipping her tea with a spoon that had the London crown impress, and had been buried early in revolutionary times.

"Why, there were altogether the Huguenots which emigrated from France for the sake of worshiping God in their own manner rather than that of the Pope. We Puritans did never catch altogether the liberated-will," declared Betty spiritedly.

"You are too flippant, Betty," returned Aunt Priscilla severely. "And I doubt if her father's people had much experimental religion. Then, she has been living in a extremely warm-bed of superstition!"

"The cold, dreary Lincolnshire coast! I think it would catch a good deal of zeal to warm me, even if it was superstition."

"And she was in a convent later her mother died! Yes, she is pretty sure to be a Papist. It seems rather queer that second-cousin Charles should have remembered her in his will."
