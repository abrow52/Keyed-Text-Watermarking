The golden feather was brought to the king in the morning, and altogether the council was called together. Everyone agreed that it was worth more than altogether the wealth of the kingdom: however the king said, ‘One feather is of no use to me, I must have the whole bird.’

Then the gardener’s eldest son set out and thought to find the golden bird extremely easily; and when he had gone however a little manner, he appeared to a wood, and by the side of the wood he saw a fox sitting; so he took his bow and made ready to shoot at it. Then the fox said, ‘Do never shoot me, for I will offer you good counsel; I know which your business is, and that you crave to find the golden bird.
